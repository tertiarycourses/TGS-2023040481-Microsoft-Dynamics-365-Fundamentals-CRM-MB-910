# Lab 9 - Dataverse, Power Platform, Activities, Views, Charts, and Dashboards

## Objective

Describe Microsoft Dataverse, Power Platform, customers, activities, search, views, charts, dashboards, and reporting in Dynamics 365 customer engagement apps.

## Scenario

Contoso managers want to understand how Dynamics 365 apps share data and how users can search, filter, report, and act on customer records.

## Prerequisites

- Review Microsoft Dataverse concepts.
- Review Power Platform and model-driven apps.
- Review Dynamics 365 reporting concepts.

## Tasks

### Step 1 - Describe Dataverse tables

Create a table map:

| Business concept | Dataverse table idea | Used by |
|------------------|----------------------|---------|

Include:

- Account
- Contact
- Lead
- Opportunity
- Case
- Work order
- Activity

### Step 2 - Map Power Platform relationships

Describe how these relate:

- Dynamics 365 apps
- Dataverse
- Power Apps
- Power Automate
- Power BI
- Copilot Studio

Write a short paragraph explaining why Dataverse is the foundation.

### Step 3 - Define activities

Create examples for:

- Phone call
- Email
- Appointment
- Task
- Note

For each, identify which CRM process might use it.

### Step 4 - Design views and filters

Create three views:

| View name | Table | Filter criteria | User |
|-----------|-------|-----------------|------|

Examples:

- My open opportunities
- High priority cases
- Work orders scheduled today

### Step 5 - Design charts and dashboards

Create dashboard requirements:

| Dashboard | Charts | Audience | Decision supported |
|-----------|--------|----------|--------------------|

Include sales, service, and field service dashboards.

## Deliverable

Submit:

- Dataverse table map
- Power Platform relationship notes
- Activity examples
- View/filter table
- Dashboard requirements

## Checkpoint questions

1. Why is Dataverse important to customer engagement apps?
2. What is the difference between a view and a chart?
3. How can dashboards support management decisions?
